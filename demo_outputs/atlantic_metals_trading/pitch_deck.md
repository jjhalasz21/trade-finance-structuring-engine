# Atlantic Metals Trading – Trade Finance Solution
*HSBC Global Trade Solutions | January 2025*

---

## Executive Summary

- **Problem:** Atlantic Metals holds $145mm of LME-listed copper and aluminum inventory but faces a $100mm working capital gap. Current banking relationships (ING, Rabobank) are not fully meeting liquidity needs, and the client's high obligor concentration (top-3 counterparties = 100% of receivables) limits traditional receivables finance appeal.

- **HSBC Proposal:** A $97mm non-recourse borrowing-base inventory facility secured by physical copper and aluminum in HSBC-controlled warehouses, with independent inspection, LME-hedging capability, and collateral monitoring.

- **Client Benefit:** Closes the $100mm WC gap with $72.75mm drawn at facility inception. Unlocks liquidity from highly liquid, exchange-traded commodities without balance-sheet recourse. Pricing of SOFR + 250bps + 50bps fee reflects operational control and commodity-grade collateral quality.

---

## Client Diagnostic

Atlantic Metals is a $800mm-revenue base metals trader with a concentrated obligor base (Glencore, Trafigura, and Louis Dreyfus account for 100% of receivables). Payment terms average 60 days, creating a working capital cycle stress (CCC stress score: 0.60). The client's $145mm inventory position—held in LME-quality copper and aluminum—is the primary liquidity lever. Obligor concentration risk (score: 0.85) makes traditional accounts receivable finance less attractive; however, the counterparties are all investment-grade trading houses (BBB/A rated), and the client has explicitly stated no appetite for off-balance-sheet structures. The inventory itself is the optimal collateral pool: it is physically stored in approved warehouses, hedgeable on the LME, and represents 18% of annual revenue—a material but manageable concentration.

---

## Proposed Solution

**Borrowing-Base Inventory Facility – $97mm Limit**

This is a non-recourse, uncommitted facility secured by LME-listed copper and aluminum held in HSBC-controlled warehouse receipts. The 67.5% advance rate reflects commodity-specific haircuts (typically 15–20% for base metals) and a 1% price haircut in lieu of a dilution reserve, given the exchange-traded nature and daily mark-to-market capability of the collateral. The facility is sized to close Atlantic Metals' $100mm working capital gap while maintaining prudent advance rates against a volatile commodity pool. Pricing of SOFR + 250bps + 50bps (unutilized fee) compensates HSBC for collateral monitoring complexity and commodity price risk, while the 50bps fee incentivizes disciplined draw behavior.
